# Arcane Forge

Melt magic weapons and armor into **arcane dust**, and a blacksmith turns that dust into **slayer oil**. Rub the oil into any weapon and it hits like a slayer until the oil wears off.

## How It Works

1. **Melt.** A blacksmith melts magic weapons, armor and shields at an Arcane Forge into arcane dust.
2. **Hunt.** Monsters drop a reagent for their own slayer group. Undead drop grave marrow, dragons and serpents drop scale ichor, and so on.
3. **Craft.** A blacksmith combines dust, reagents, ingots and gold into a slayer oil.
4. **Coat.** Double-click the oil and target your weapon. It now counts as that slayer until its charges run out.

## Melting at the Arcane Forge

Arcane Forges stand in a few towns. To use one you need at least **90 Blacksmithy**.

Double-click the forge and target a magic weapon, armor piece or shield in your backpack. After a few seconds it dissolves into arcane dust.

- The item must be **identified** first
- Fuel costs **50 gold per dust** the item is worth in the table below, so a Ruin weapon costs 50 gold and a Vanquishing weapon 250
- You can melt **250 dust's worth per day**, such as 50 Vanquishing weapons or 250 Ruin weapons. This is per player across all your accounts, and the day is the last 24 hours, not a calendar day
- The forge won't take slayer weapons, blessed or newbied items, or artifacts
- Melting a coated weapon destroys its coating. The forge warns you first and asks you to target the weapon again

### Dust Yield

Better magic gives more dust. Weapons count their damage or accuracy level, whichever is higher. Armor and shields count their protection level. Items that only have a durability bonus count as the lowest level.

| Weapon Level |   Armor Level   | Average Dust |
| :----------: | :-------------: | :----------: |
|     Ruin     |     Defense     |      1       |
|    Might     |    Guarding     |      1       |
|    Force     |    Hardening    |      2       |
|    Power     |  Fortification  |      3       |
| Vanquishing  | Invulnerability |      5       |

Every melt varies a little, so you may get somewhat more or less than the average.

**Alchemy** makes a real difference. With no Alchemy you get about 20% less than the table, and a Grandmaster Alchemist gets about 20% more. A GM smith who is also a GM alchemist averages 6 dust from a Vanquishing weapon. Every melt gives at least 1 dust, so Ruin and Might items give 1 even with no Alchemy. Melting also gives you a chance to gain Alchemy.

## Slayer Reagents

Every slayer group has its own reagent. Monsters in that group have a chance to drop it when they die, and the tougher the monster, the better the chance. A skeleton or snake rarely drops one, while dragons, daemons and liches drop them often enough that hunting them with an oil earns back most of the reagents for the next one. Reagents also drop more often in a dungeon that is running the **Boon of the Slayer** bonus that week. See [Dungeon of the Week](dotw.md).

Champion spawn monsters and pets don't drop reagents.

|    Reagent     |                                      Dropped By                                      |        Makes         |
| :------------: | :----------------------------------------------------------------------------------: | :------------------: |
|  Grave Marrow  |                 Undead: skeletons, zombies, liches, wraiths, mummies                 |  Silver Slayer Oil   |
| Brute's Blood  | Humanoids: orcs, ogres, trolls, ettins, cyclopes, ratmen, savages, pirates, brigands |  Repond Slayer Oil   |
|  Infernal Ash  |        Daemons and gargoyles: daemons, balrons, imps, ice fiends, gargoyles         | Exorcism Slayer Oil  |
| Elemental Core |     Elementals: air, earth, fire, water, blood, poison, ore elementals, efreets      | Elemental Slayer Oil |
|   Venom Sac    |     Arachnids: giant spiders, dread spiders, black widows, scorpions, terathans      | Arachnid Slayer Oil  |
|  Scale Ichor   |      Reptiles: dragons, drakes, wyverns, serpents, snakes, lizardmen, ophidians      | Reptilian Slayer Oil |

## Crafting Slayer Oil

Slayer oils are under **Slayer Oils** in the blacksmith crafting menu. Like anything else you smith, you need to be near an anvil and a forge.

Every oil needs:

- **90 Blacksmithy** and **70 Alchemy**
- **20 arcane dust**
- **3 of the matching reagent**
- **10 ingots**
- **5,000 gold**

Your chance starts at nothing at 90 Blacksmithy and climbs to about 95% at Grandmaster. Anything that puts you past 100 means you never fail. A failed attempt loses the dust, reagents and ingots, but you keep the gold.

## Coating a Weapon

Double-click a slayer oil and target a weapon in your backpack or hands. The weapon now counts as that slayer.

- A coating holds **250** slayer hits, or **300** on an exceptional weapon
- A charge is only used when you hit a monster the oil slays. Hits on anything else are free, and so is travel, recalling or standing around
- Single-click the weapon to see what's left, for example **[silver oil: 212]**
- Coating with the same oil again resets it to full; it doesn't add to what's left. A different oil replaces the old coating. Either way the oil warns you first and asks you to target the weapon again
- Weapons that already have a slayer can't be coated
- The coating stays on the weapon. If someone loots it, they get whatever charges were left

### The Slayer's Drawback

A coating carries the same drawback as a real slayer. While you wield it, monsters from the opposing group hit you for **double damage** with their weapons, claws and arrows. Their spells don't hit any harder. The oil tells you which group to watch out for when you coat. Know where you're hunting before you coat.

|    Oil    | Opposing Group (hits you harder) |
| :-------: | :------------------------------: |
|  Silver   |            Humanoids             |
|  Repond   |              Undead              |
| Exorcism  |            Elementals            |
| Elemental |      Daemons and gargoyles       |
| Arachnid  |             Reptiles             |
| Reptilian |            Arachnids             |

Slayer oil only works on monsters. It has no effect on other players or on their pets and summons, in either direction: a coated weapon doesn't slay a tamer's dragon, and a summoned daemon doesn't hit you harder for the oil you carry.
